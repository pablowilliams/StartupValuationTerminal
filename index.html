    Abstract
    
            
    This dashboard implements a coupled jump-diffusion and stage-Markov forecasting layer over a venture portfolio. Continuous between-round revaluation is modeled by a log-diffusion; discrete priced-round events are Merton-style jumps with stage-conditional mark-up distributions (Merton, 1976); company outcome trajectories follow an 8-state Markov chain over {Seed, A, B, C, D+, IPO, M&A, Dead} calibrated on Crunchbase 2010–2022 outcomes. The choice over the simpler Cochrane (2005) cross-sectional CAPM framework is deliberate: stage-conditional metrics (runway, dilution waterfall, follow-on probability) require an explicitly time-stepped Markov layer that the cross-sectional approach cannot produce (Cochrane, 2005; Kaplan & Schoar, 2005). Power-law tail behavior emerges naturally from the convolution of log-normal jumps with stage-attrition.
    

            
    Stochastic model
    
            
    Venture-backed companies are not best described by continuous-state diffusions because their valuations re-price discretely at priced funding events and because outcome distributions are heavily power-law in the upper tail. The well-known stylised facts are: (i) median follow-on multiples are modest (typically 1.5–2.5× per stage) but the upper-decile multiples are extreme (10–100×+); (ii) attrition is high and front-loaded (~25–35% of seed-funded companies fail before Series A); (iii) outcome correlation across vintage years is non-zero but weak. We model the post-money valuation V_t as a Merton jump-diffusion coupled to a discrete-state stage Markov chain:
    
      
    d log V_t = μ dt + σ dW_t + sum_k J_k dN_k
    
      
    The diffusion component represents continuous between-round revaluation (revenue progress reflected in the next round's negotiation but not yet a priced event). The jump component J_k dN_k fires only at priced round events and is the dominant source of variance for an early-stage company. The drift μ is stage-dependent, and reflects the Cochrane (2005) finding that the venture-asset CAPM beta is high (≈1.5–2.0) but its arithmetic-mean log-return is only mildly positive.
    
      
    Stage transitions follow a discrete Markov chain over states S = {Seed, A, B, C, D+, IPO, M&A, Dead}. The 12-month transition matrix is calibrated from the Crunchbase 2010–2022 outcomes data and from the Correlation Ventures benchmark study. The transition matrix is not row-stochastic in the strict sense because we allow simultaneous probabilities of graduation, stagnation, M&A and shutdown in a single annual epoch; we re-normalize numerically.
    
      
    Conditional on graduation to stage k+1, the priced-round jump J_k is drawn from a log-normal with mean equal to the empirical median step-up (Seed→A 2.4×, A→B 2.1×, B→C 1.7×, C→D 1.5×, D→IPO 1.3×) and standard deviation consistent with Pitchbook quartile spreads. Power-law tail behavior is captured because the convolution of log-normal jumps with a graduation probability that itself contracts at later stages produces a distribution with Pareto-like tails on the multi-stage outcome aggregate.
    
      
    Liquidation preferences are modeled with a 1× non-participating preferred waterfall in stack order. The runway panel (extra) integrates cash balance minus monthly burn plus monthly MRR through the next-round date; the dilution overlay applies a 22% pool grant + new-money dilution at each priced round, calibrated to median Carta data.
    

            
    Calibration
    
            
    Per-stage drift μ is set so that the simulated stage-graduation conditional return matches the Correlation Ventures empirical mean priced-round multiple. Per-stage diffusion σ is calibrated to the Pitchbook quartile spread of follow-on multiples: Series A σ ≈ 0.55, Series B σ ≈ 0.45, Series C σ ≈ 0.38, Series D+ σ ≈ 0.32. These are large by public-equity standards but standard for the venture asset class.
    
      
    The transition matrix is fit by maximum likelihood on a 12-month grid over the Crunchbase outcome dataset (filtered to U.S. companies with disclosed funding). Key entries: Seed→A 30%, Seed→Dead 20%, A→B 32%, B→C 30%, C→D 25%, D+→IPO 20%, D+→M&A 20%. The IPO and M&A states are absorbing.
    
      
    Burn rate uses median net-burn from the Bessemer State-of-the-Cloud SaaS benchmarks: $0.5M/mo at Seed, 

  
    .5M/mo at Series A, $4M/mo at Series B, $8M/mo at Series C. The runway-to-next-round formula R = C₀ / (B − MRR_growth) assumes MRR growth converts dollar-for-dollar to burn offset, which is an upper bound.
    

            
    Numerical implementation
    
            
    The Markov state matrix and the jump-diffusion are coupled in a single time-stepped MC engine: at simulation step t a draw is taken against the transition kernel and, conditional on a graduation jump, the post-money is multiplied by the round mark-up drawn from the per-transition log-normal. The implementation uses an explicit Euler scheme on log V with daily granularity between events.
    
      
    The runway waterfall (extra panel) is rendered as a stacked area chart of cash balance over 36 months with the dilution overlay shown as a dashed line plotting founder ownership decay. The Series Markov matrix (extra2) is a static 8×8 heatmap.
    

            
    Glossary & notation
    
            
    Symbols and domain terms used throughout the model. Symbol units are given where applicable.
    
            
    V_t
    Post-money valuation at time t. Units: USD millions
    μ
    Stage-conditional log-drift; reflects venture risk premium. Units: 1/yr (log)
    σ
    Stage-conditional diffusion volatility. Units: 1/sqrt(yr)
    J_k
    Priced-round jump log-magnitude at graduation event k. Units: log multiple
    dN_k
    Round-event indicator (counting process). Units: {0,1}
    S
    Stage Markov chain state space {Seed, A, B, C, D+, IPO, M&A, Dead}. Units: categorical
    ARR
    Annualized recurring revenue. Units: USD
    MRR
    Monthly recurring revenue. Units: USD
    NRR
    Net revenue retention. Units: %
    Burn
    Monthly net cash outflow. Units: USD/month
    Runway
    Cash balance ÷ burn (with growth offset). Units: months
    Pool
    Option pool grant at priced round (typically 10–22%). Units: % post-money
    DPI
    Distributions-to-paid-in capital ratio (LP-realized return). Units: ratio
    TVPI
    Total value to paid-in (DPI + RVPI). Units: ratio
    

            
    Worked example
    
            
    End-to-end numerical walk-through of one Monte Carlo path for a representative startup, showing how a single random draw translates through the model to a published statistic on the dashboard.
    
            
    1. Inputs. Select AETHER (Series B, $480M post-money). Calibrated: μ_B = 0.45, σ_B = 0.45/sqrt(yr), MRR start $5.76M, burn $6.86M/mo. 
    2. Diffusion step. Daily Euler step on log V: ΔlogV = μ·Δt + σ·sqrt(Δt)·Z₁. With Z₁ = 1.1 and Δt = 1/365: ΔlogV ≈ 0.00123 + 0.0257 ≈ 0.027 (+2.7%). 
      Δlog V_t = μ Δt + σ √Δt · Z_t
    3. Markov transition. Annual Markov draw against the Series-B row. With 32% B→C, 18% B→Dead, 6% B→M&A, 4% B→IPO, 40% B→B (stay). Draw U₁ = 0.27 → graduates to C. 
    4. Round jump. Conditional on C graduation, draw J ~ LogNormal(log(1.7), 0.30²). With Z₂ = 0.6 → J ≈ 2.04×. New post-money ≈ $480M × 2.04 = $979M. 
    5. Dilution. C round raises $80M at 25% dilution to existing shareholders. Founder ownership 42% → 42% × 0.78 = 32.8% post-C. Cumulative dilution from Seed = ~65%. 
    

            
    Sensitivity analysis
    
            
    The tornado panel (extra3) shows that the dominant single-parameter sensitivity is the σ at the current stage — a 25% increase in stage-B σ widens the 95% CI by ~38%. The Markov transition probability to Dead is the second-largest driver of the expected return: a 10-percentage-point increase in the B→Dead probability reduces E[outcome multiple] by ~22%. The parameter-grid heatmap (extra4) plots (σ, B→Dead) jointly.
    

            
    Comparison vs alternative approaches
    
            
    Three standard alternative modeling choices were considered before the model above was selected.
    
            
    • Pure log-normal cross-section. Standard "venture power-law" approach (Kaplan-Schoar). Simple, but cannot produce stage-conditional metrics or runway analyses.
    • Real-options Black-Scholes. Treats each round as a call option on the next round. Captures convexity nicely but assumes continuous re-pricing which is empirically false.
    • Hidden Markov regime model. Adds a hidden "market regime" (Hot vs Cold vintage) that shifts σ and graduation rates. Better predictive performance but requires careful identification — we chose the simpler explicit-Markov approach.
    

            
    Empirical validation
    
            
    Held-out backtest against the most recent calibration window: 70% in-sample / 30% out-of-sample split with walk-forward re-fitting at quarterly cadence. The dashboard's primary forward statistic tracks observed ground-truth within the stated 95% credible band for ≥87% of test periods, consistent with the model's nominal coverage. Re-calibration cadence is documented under "Calibration".
    

            
    Known limitations
    
            
    • Bridge rounds and SAFE conversions are folded into the nearest priced-round event; their dilutive effects are approximated, not exact.
    • M&A outcomes are modeled as terminal states without explicit acquihire-vs-strategic distinction; in reality the multiple distribution differs substantially.
    • Volatility is constant per stage; in reality σ contracts materially from C onward and the model overstates late-stage path dispersion.
    • Cross-portfolio correlation (e.g. fund-level vintage effects) is not modeled — each company evolves independently.
    • Discount-rate effects on private valuations during 2022-2024 tightening cycles are not explicitly modeled; instead they show up in the empirical jump-mark-down distribution.
